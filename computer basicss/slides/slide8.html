Internet Basics

The internet connects computers worldwide. Learn how browsers, addresses and connectivity fit together.

  • URL & Address — https://example.com tells the browser where to go.
  • ISP & Router — your internet provider and local device that shares the link.
  • Wi‑Fi vs Wired — faster, more stable with cable; Wi‑Fi gives mobility.
Practice layer

Break down a web address, for example https://dericbi.vercel.app/courses:

  1. https: the secure way to talk to the site.
  2. dericbi.vercel.app: the domain, the site's name.
  3. /courses: the page inside the site.

Your phone or computer reaches the internet through a router supplied by your ISP, or through mobile data. Test: open Settings > Network & internet; you should see Connected. Slip: Wi-Fi connected but no internet; restart the router, wait two minutes.

Level-up layer

The padlock or "https" means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest; scam sites can use it too. Always read the domain carefully, since mpesa-support.example is not Safaricom. Wired is more stable than Wi-Fi; sit closer to the router and avoid walls. Know the difference between slow Wi-Fi and a slow site by testing another site. Mobile data bundles run out quickly on video and updates.

Check yourself: Your laptop shows Wi-Fi connected but no website opens. What do you try first?

Answer: Restart the router and reconnect, then test another site or device to see if the problem is the connection or one website.
