Intro to AI for Everyday Tasks

Artificial Intelligence (AI) can help you work faster, learn smarter, and automate simple tasks — even without programming skills.

  • What is AI? Tools that analyze data, understand language, or generate content (text, images, audio).
  • Everyday examples: auto-summarizing long notes, drafting emails, extracting key facts from a document, or generating simple images.
  • How it helps: saves time on repetitive work, offers writing and design suggestions, and assists decision making.

Safe & smart use

  1. Verify outputs—AI can be confident but wrong; always check facts.
  2. Protect sensitive data—don't paste private information into online tools.
  3. Combine AI with your judgment—use it to speed up, not replace critical thinking.

Practice layer

Use AI as a drafting helper, step by step:

  1. Check what is currently available and read its privacy terms.
  2. Write a clear prompt: "Write a polite SMS reminding customers their order is ready. Max 30 words. Friendly tone."
  3. Read the answer and edit it so it sounds like you.
  4. Verify any facts, numbers or names yourself.

Never paste customer phone numbers, M-Pesa statements or passwords into public AI tools. Use sample or anonymised text instead.

Level-up layer

AI can sound confident while being wrong, so treat it as a first draft from a quick assistant, not an authority. Good prompts state the task, audience, tone, length and an example. If the answer is weak, say what to change instead of starting over. For anything about tax, health or law, confirm with an official source. Keep a notes file of prompts that worked for you. Use AI to learn: ask it to explain a concept simply, then test yourself.

Check yourself: Why should you not paste a list of customer names and phone numbers into a public AI tool?

Answer: It is private information you may not be allowed to share, and you may lose control of where it goes. Anonymise or use made-up examples.
