3 Rules of Clean Data

Follow these rules to keep your data reliable and useful.

Rule 1: Be Consistent

Use the same format for dates, names, and numbers.

Consistency avoids confusion

Rule 2: Be Accurate

Double-check entries to avoid errors.

Accuracy builds trust

Rule 3: Be Complete

Fill in all required fields.

No gaps in your data

Practice layer

Fix a sample sheet to follow all three rules:

  1. Consistent: set the Date column to one format, for example 2023-01-05 (year-month-day sorts correctly).
  2. Accurate: re-check every amount against the receipt; compare =SUM(C2:C50) with your till total.
  3. Complete: filter the column for blanks and fill or mark each one.

Example: "shoes", "Shoes " and "SHOES" are three different items to Excel. Pick Shoes, then use Find & Replace to make all rows match. Totals by item will then add up correctly.

Level-up layer

The rules work together: a consistent but inaccurate sheet is confidently wrong. Pros apply them at the moment of entry, because fixing later costs far more. Useful tools: Data Validation dropdowns for items, and a rule that no row is saved without all columns. Watch for invisible spaces at the end of names. Write your three rules down and put them where you record. Next, study the mistakes that break each rule.

Check yourself: Why do "shoes", "Shoes " and "SHOES" cause wrong totals?

Answer: Spreadsheets and pivots can treat each spelling or trailing space as a separate item, so one product is split into several rows. Standardise the name at entry.
