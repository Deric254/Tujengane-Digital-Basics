What Is Clean Data?

Clean data is accurate, consistent, and complete, ready to fuel your business decisions.

  • Accurate: No errors or duplicates
    Correct data builds trust
  • Consistent: Uniform formats
    Same style across records
  • Complete: No missing info
    Every field filled correctly

Practice layer

Test whether a sheet is clean with three quick checks on a Date, Item, Amount table:

  1. Accurate: does =COUNTA(A2:A100) equal the number of sales you really made?
  2. Consistent: do all dates look alike, and are amounts numbers (right-aligned), not text?
  3. Complete: does =COUNTBLANK(A2:C100) return 0?

Example: a row reading "5k" in the Amount column fails consistency, because Excel cannot add "5k". Replace it with 5000. One blank amount fails completeness, so find the receipt and fill it.

Level-up layer

Clean does not mean perfect; it means fit for the decision you are making. A sheet can be accurate but inconsistent (the right amounts, written three ways) and still give wrong totals. Pros add a fourth idea: timely, because last month's stock count is not today's. Build checks into the sheet itself, such as dropdown lists and number-only columns, so errors are blocked at entry. Check yourself: A sheet has all amounts filled in and correct, but dates are written as "1/1/23", "Jan 1" and "1st January". Which quality is missing?

Answer: Consistency. The values are accurate and complete, but mixed formats break sorting, filtering and monthly totals.
