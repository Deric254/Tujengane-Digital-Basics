Practice layer

See the problem in numbers. A shop owner thinks she sold 40 bags this week. The notebook says 32, the M-Pesa messages show 36 payments, and the stock count implies 38.

  1. List each source: notebook, M-Pesa, stock.
  2. Find the gap: 32 vs 36 vs 38.
  3. Ask why each differs (credit sales, forgotten entries, returns).

With no single reliable record she cannot know her real sales, so she cannot know her profit. The fix is not fancy software; it is one place where every sale is written the same way, every day.

Level-up layer

Notice that three records disagreeing is normal, not a sign of dishonesty. Experts reconcile sources instead of choosing one. Start by agreeing one "source of truth" for each fact: sales from the till book, payments from M-Pesa, stock from counts. Differences then become questions to investigate. Be careful with "we have always done it this way": old habits hide leaks. Next, learn what clean data looks like so you can build that single record.

Check yourself: Your notebook shows 32 sales but M-Pesa shows 36 payments. Name two harmless reasons and one worrying reason.

Answer: Harmless: payments for deposits or debts, or sales written under a different date. Worrying: sales taken and never recorded. Reconcile line by line to find out which.
