Case Study: Jane's Boutique

Jane, a Kenyan boutique owner, asked DericBI to analyze her sales data. The results were shocking!

  • Her best-selling item was not what she thought
    Data revealed hidden trends
  • Most sales happened on weekdays, not weekends
    Changed her marketing strategy
  • She had repeat customers from a new area
    Expanded her delivery zone
  • Profit increased by 30% after cleaning her data
    Clean data = better decisions

Practice layer

Reproduce Jane's analysis with your own sales sheet:

  1. Insert a column Weekday with =TEXT(A2,"dddd").
  2. Build a PivotTable: Weekday in Rows, Amount in Values (Sum).
  3. Second pivot: Item in Rows, Amount (Sum), sorted largest first.
  4. Add Area or Location to Rows to spot repeat customers by area.

Compare the answers with your gut feeling. The gap between belief and numbers is where the profit is.

Level-up layer

Be careful reading the story: sales on weekdays might simply mean the shop opens more weekdays, so compare average per open day, not just total. Check that the best seller by quantity is also best by profit. The case works because her data was clean first; analysis on messy records would have misled her. Next step: Excel pivots, then dashboards, so findings update without rebuilding.

Check yourself: Jane finds most sales are on weekdays. Why compare average sales per day rather than totals before changing marketing?

Answer: Weekdays outnumber weekend days, so totals favour them. Averages per open day show true daily performance before you act.
