Practice layer

Follow one error through to money. Your sheet says 12 packets of unga are in stock, but 4 were sold and not recorded. Real stock is 8.

  1. A customer wants 10 packets; you promise them.
  2. You can supply only 8, so you lose 2 sales and disappoint the customer.
  3. Or you reorder based on false numbers and tie up cash.

Fix: a quick physical count weekly on top items, compared with the sheet, and corrected the same day.

Level-up layer

Errors cost in two directions: overstated stock loses sales, understated stock ties up cash. Experts track the biggest items first, using the 80/20 idea that a few products drive most of the turnover. Count the top ten weekly and the rest monthly. Record the reason for each difference (theft, spoilage, unrecorded sale). Patterns in reasons point to the real problem. Next, build the daily recording habit that prevents this.

Check yourself: Your sheet shows more stock than you actually have. What business risks follow?

Answer: You may promise goods you cannot supply, lose sales and trust, or mis-plan reorders. Regular counts compared against the sheet catch this early.
