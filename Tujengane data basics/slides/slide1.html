Practice layer

Before you start, pick one real thing you track, for example daily duka sales. In this one-day session you will do three things:

  1. Learn what makes a record trustworthy.
  2. Practise the three rules on a sample sheet.
  3. Audit your own records and fix one problem today.

Have a notebook or phone spreadsheet ready. A good goal for the day is one sentence such as: "By tonight my sales sheet will have one date format and no blank amounts." Small, checkable goals beat big vague ones.

Level-up layer

Beginners often wait to "get organised" before collecting data. Do the opposite: start recording imperfectly today, then clean as you learn. Experts treat data as a habit, not a project. Bring your own messy records to the exercises, because you learn faster on data you care about. After this session, the natural next step is a spreadsheet tool, since clean data is only useful once you can total, sort and compare it.

Check yourself: What one concrete outcome do you want from your own records by the end of the day?

Answer: Any specific, checkable goal works, for example "one date format and no blank amounts in my sales sheet". A goal you can verify tonight is better than "be more organised".
