Practice layer

Compare the two sheets from the slide using three tests:

  1. Sort by Date. The clean sheet sorts correctly; "Jan 1" does not become a proper date.
  2. Add the Amount column: =SUM(C2:C4). The clean sheet gives 8000; "5k" is text and is ignored, so the messy total is wrong.
  3. Find the row with no date: you cannot say when that sale happened.

Repair: change "5k" to 5000, "Jan 1" to 2023-01-01, capitalise "shoes", and trace the missing date to a receipt.

Level-up layer

Notice that the messy sheet looks readable to a human but is unreadable to a computer. That gap is the heart of data quality. Excel signals text-numbers with a green triangle or left-aligned values; look for them. Use Text to Columns, VALUE() or TRIM() for bulk repairs. Better still, prevent mess with validation. Keep the original messy file untouched, and clean a copy so you can always trace changes. Next, audit your own sheet.

Check yourself: In the messy sheet, why does =SUM ignore the "5k" amount?

Answer: "5k" is text, not a number, and SUM only adds true numbers. It must be typed as 5000.
